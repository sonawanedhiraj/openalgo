# Strategy Registry — Master Index

**This is the canonical record of every trading strategy tested in this project.**

When the user says "trading strategy" — including questions like "what should I try next", "how did X do", or "is Y worth testing" — Claude should READ THIS FILE FIRST. It contains: (1) the active deployable shortlist, (2) every strategy ever rejected with WHY, (3) currently-in-flight experiments, (4) the backlog of untested ideas, and (5) the standard testing protocol so new rounds are comparable.

This is a LIVING document. After every new backtest round, add an entry. After every weekly trading session, update active strategies' learnings.

## How to use this file

**Finding past work:** Search by strategy name (e.g. "bull flag") or round number ("round 13"). If a strategy was tried before, it will be in "Rejected" or "Active." If it is not in any list, it is a fresh idea — see "Backlog" for queued ideas, and consider testing.

**Adding a new round:** Copy the template from "## Entry Template" at the bottom. Fill it in. Add the report path. Save and commit.

**Updating an active strategy after a live session:** Open the strategy's own `strategies/<name>/LEARNINGS.md` and append daily/weekly observations there. Then update this registry's "Status / Latest Note" for that strategy.

A note on numbering: the backtest reports are numbered from Round 2 onward. There is no file named `BACKTEST_ROUND1`; the round-1-equivalent work is the initial F&O screener sweep captured in `BACKTEST_RESEARCH_REPORT_2026-06-05.md` (its cost model and harness are referenced by later reports as "round-1"). Two early experiments — the research sweep and the sector-movers study — predate the numbered series but are recorded here as full rows.

## Cross-Cutting Findings

### Bar-close-confirmed gates make trigger-level entries look-ahead (2026-07-19)

If an entry gate needs the **completed** bar to confirm (bar volume, bar close vs a level, any full-bar statistic), the backtest entry price must be that bar's **close** (or the next bar's open) — pricing the entry at the intra-bar trigger level is **look-ahead**: the level printed *before the signal existed*. On volume-burst bars the artifact is large — R58's opening vol-breakout showed a mean adverse intra-bar drift of **+0.54%/trade (median +0.28%)**, bigger than the strategy's entire claimed edge (+0.38%). Fixing the convention flipped BOTH R58 legs from positive to REJECT (equity +0.378% → −0.163%/trade; options +₹91.7k → −₹160k full-history) and reconciled BS-priced options with real broker July results (sign-agreement 67% → 92%). Corollary for options overlays: the artifact is levered — the option premium at the trigger level vs at bar close can differ 10–30%, so a look-ahead equity entry becomes a much larger look-ahead option entry. Audit check for any breakout-style round: report `mean(entry_bar_close / trigger_level − 1)` signed by side; if it is comparable to the per-trade edge, the round is measuring its own look-ahead. (Round 58 calibration, issue #424.)

### Long-gross-positive as a viability screen for intraday strategies (2026-06-07)

After 28 rejected intraday rounds + the R28b breakdown analysis:

Across the 10 intraday rounds with trade-level CSVs, longs beat shorts gross 7 of 10 times — but the actionable discriminant is NOT long-vs-short. It is whether the **long book has positive gross-before-costs**. Where long gross is positive, stripping shorts + correcting slippage to the platform's 0.035%/side default flips net green; where it is negative (R1, R2, R13 bull-flag, R15 mean-reversion, R28 v1), nothing rescues the round.

**Re-examine under a "long-only + score-positive" lens:**
- R3, R4, R5 (sector-engine variants — small samples, ~10-13 trades).
- R28b v2 Sneaky Pivot (73-trade long book, +24k gross → +5k net at 0.035% slip, tightening to ~+2-3k net after this PR's brokerage cap fix).

**Confirmed dead — long gross already negative:** R1, R2, R13, R15, R28 v1.

**Caveats:**
1. The per-leg brokerage cap fix in PR #2 adds ~25% cost on mid-size intraday trades; estimates above use the corrected formula.
2. None of the re-examination candidates clear the deployable bar — they are statistical noise (+2 to +7k over 5 months). This is a screening rule, not a strategy.
3. Slippage remains unverified; `ltp_at_signal` capture (this PR) enables validation once ~10 real trades accumulate.

**Action for future intraday rounds:** report BOTH long-only and short-only gross/net in the trade summary so the long-gross sign is immediately visible.

### Options leverage-rescue does NOT save low-WR *intraday* strategies (2026-06-07)

Tested the operator's hypothesis that a low-win-rate equity book (R29 v2: OOS WR 34%, payoff 1.10, net -45k) could be rescued by trading **ATM calls** instead of stock — the idea being that options' asymmetric payoff (capped premium loss, open upside via delta/gamma) makes low WR survivable if payoff clears ~2.33.

**It made things worse.** Replaying all 90 signals as intraday ATM monthly calls (entry 9:45, exit 15:15, same day), priced from real NSE bhavcopy daily open/settle anchors with Black-Scholes intraday interpolation (87/90 mapped, calibration clean, median IV 30%, open-vs-settle IV spread 2%), the options book posts **OOS payoff 0.95 — below the 2.33 needed and below equity's own 1.10** — and is net negative at 10/25/50 bps (−97.6k/−102.2k/−109.8k). WR 32.8%, Sharpe −1.49.

**Why:** an intraday ATM call (~0.5 delta) held ~5.5h captures only ~half of an already-small underlying move while paying full time premium and option-specific costs (STT on sell premium, slippage on premium). Convexity needs a *large* move to bend the payoff above linear; intraday wins here are ~0.3–0.5%. So the option just tracks spot at higher cost.

**Rule:** options leverage cannot rescue an *intraday* signal with no gross edge — there is no time for gamma to work. **It CAN partially rescue a multi-day swing with real gross edge — but fragile.** Same test on **R8** (longs-only +4% swing, equity payoff 1.51, net +43k) replayed as ATM monthly calls (85/86 mapped): under faithful intraday execution the BS model lifts payoff 1.51→2.30 and net to +205k @25bps → **PROMISING** (just shy of the 2.33 DEPLOY bar). BUT a both-legs-real-EOD-settle cross-check collapses the hold≥1 edge to ≈₹0 — the result hinges on capturing premium at the *exact* intraday target instant plus constant-IV; theta/IV-path over a multi-day hold eats it otherwise. So the swing-vs-intraday distinction is the real finding (convexity needs a big move), but R8 options is execution-fragile, not deployable — needs ~10 real paper fills to measure intraday-exit slippage + IV crush before trusting the magnitude.

New reusable asset: `fo_bhavcopy_eod` in `historify.duckdb` (4.7M expired-option EOD rows; 30-symbol R29 universe over 2024-01→2025-11 + 2026-01→05, plus all-symbol coverage on R8's 55 dates) recovers expired-contract daily prices that Kite's master cache purges. Pipeline: `outputs/r29v2_options_hybrid_2026-06-07/`.

### The monthly-consistency wall is signal FREQUENCY, not signal QUALITY (R30 -> R34, 2026-06-08)

Across R30–R34 every *single-signal* strategy carrying a real edge fails the strict monthly cash-flow bar for the same structural reason: edge-bearing Indian F&O signals are **lumpy by construction** (top-2/3 sectors, pyramided winners, episodic reversions). Widening the universe 4.7x (R31) did not raise trade frequency. Market-neutralizing (R33) stripped out the directional sector edge that made the signal worth trading. Mean-reversion (R32) has no structural single-name edge to begin with. The *only* thing that cleared the strict bar (R34 V_BLD_B, Sharpe 1.41 / 70% green) was **portfolio-layer blending of 4 uncorrelated lumpy sleeves** — monthly consistency is manufactured at the portfolio layer, not discovered in a better single signal. Next lever is portfolio-layer construction, not another signal hunt.

### Synthetic Black-Scholes pricing is systematically OPTIMISTIC for option BUYING (R36-real, 2026-06-09)

Real-data Sharpe was 0.4-1.1 worse than synthetic BS-pricing on every variant. Synthetic used IV=RV×1.10; real market IV runs above realized vol because of the implied-volatility floor, and buyers pay it. **Implication: any prior round that used synthetic BS pricing for option BUYING (R8 PROMISING, R29 v2, R35) needs an "optimistic-pricing" caveat until re-tested with real bhavcopy.** Premium SELLING (R37/R38) is unaffected — those tests used real bhavcopy throughout. Backfilled asset: `index_options_eod` in `historify.duckdb` (1.64M rows, NIFTY+BANKNIFTY, 2022-01 -> 2026-06).

### Premium selling edge exists at trade level but dies at portfolio level due to entry-gate co-occurrence (R37, 2026-06-09)

Iron condors with disciplined exits and tight strike selection produce real trade-level edge (un-breached condors hit 50% profit consistently). But the natural conjunction of entry conditions ("vix high AND range-bound AND fresh DTE window") fires far less often than the binding-evidence threshold requires (N=4 / 23 months). Weeklies / continuous-DTE / looser regime could fix it but become different strategies. SPAN margin (~5-15× max-loss) caps real-capital deployment regardless.

### Delta-roll adjustments make defined-risk condors strictly worse, not nicer (R38, 2026-06-09)

The textbook "roll the untested side when tested-delta hits 0.30" discipline manufactures a fresh directional breach risk (re-centered untested side, no longer delta-neutral). In whipsaw or vol-expansion, both sides breach → double-loss tail manufactured. Cosmetic-to-harmful on gap days specifically (0 of 13 gap-day prevents in audit). Net credit added by rolls is positive everywhere yet realized P&L falls.

### Conditional per-asset framing dies on Bonferroni; broad unconditional edge + sleeve-blending wins (R39 → R41, 2026-06-10)

Conditional-edge per-asset framing died on n+Bonferroni (R39): the per-stock conditional edge cleared 0/115 cells, vol-regime sector mean-reversion collapsed OOS, and the variance-premium signal was too rare to trade. Broad *unconditional* edge (SECTOR_FOLLOW) won instead (R40-R41) — it emerged as a standalone from R39-E's setup-library sweep and survived a real-capital concurrency cap (V_SF_CAP5_VOL). And dynamic allocation across uncorrelated sleeves beats single-strategy maximization (R34/R41 PROMOTE): adding SECTOR_FOLLOW as a 5th sleeve lifted the blend on *every* axis simultaneously (Sharpe 1.41→2.42, green 70→78%, payoff 1.67→2.27, MaxDD −3.2→−2.2%), confirming the R34 thesis that monthly consistency is manufactured at the portfolio layer, not discovered in a better single signal — and that more uncorrelated lumpy sleeves is strictly better.

### The sector_follow overnight edge is LONG-ONLY — the short mirror bounces (R42, 2026-07-05)

The inverse of futures_follow_cap50 (SHORT NIFTY futures when sector < −1.5%, stock < −0.5%, vol > 1× at the close) REJECTS decisively: CAGR +1.05% / Sharpe 0.15 with E4, CAGR −7.8% / MaxDD −25% without. Mechanism: after a down-signal day the signal stock *bounces* (+0.38% mean next-day, only 45.6% continue down) while after an up-signal day it *continues* (+0.51%, 59.2%) — panic closes in Indian large caps mean-revert overnight, and NIFTY's structural drift (+0.17%/day after bullish signals, only 48.5% down after bearish ones) turns from tailwind to headwind. E4 (skip high-vol sector regimes) is the only thing keeping the short above water, i.e. its best filter is "don't trade when the signal is strongest" — the tell that no edge exists underneath. Combined with MIS-T+0 (fails: edge is the hold, not the move) and option buying (fails: theta), every wrapper except LONG delta-1 overnight now rejects on this signal family. Report: `docs/research/strategy/futures_follow_cap50/2026-07-05_inverse_short_futures_backtest.md`.

### Hard intraday stops are net-negative on the sector_follow overnight signal family — confirmed unleveraged AND leveraged (R54, 2026-07-13)

Phase-1 found hard intraday stops net-neutral/self-defeating on the *unleveraged* ₹2.5L equity book; R54 confirmed the same **on the ~7×-leveraged NIFTY-futures wrapper**, so the finding is not a leverage artifact. Every stop distance reduces CAGR (14.84→11–13%) and Sharpe (1.31→0.95–1.14); the ones that fire make the **worst day bigger** (−₹45k to −₹69k vs −₹34k). Two mechanisms: (1) a stop can't catch the overnight gap — it fills at the gapped open; (2) on this family intraday dips frequently *recover* into the 15:25 T+1 close, so stopping out locks in a price worse than holding, and whipsaw-outs cluster into new bad days. A bad *individual* day (2026-07-07 war news, −493 pts, 75% intraday grind) would have been helped by a stop — but that is the exception; the sample rule is dip-then-recover. **The edge is the full overnight hold; stops relocate and deepen the tail rather than capping it.** Report: `docs/research/strategy/futures_follow_cap50/2026-07-13_intraday_stop_loss_backtest.md`.

### Option BUYING fails on the sector_follow overnight family — as a return vehicle AND as a hedge (R55, 2026-07-13)

R54 concluded "true gap protection needs a defined-risk OTM put, not a stop"; R55 tested that put hedge with real NIFTY bhavcopy premiums and it **REJECTS too**. Every OTM-put overnight hedge (0.5–3% OTM, full/half-notional) is a net cost and lowers **both** CAGR (15.5→7–13%) and Sharpe (1.35→0.88–1.23); it bounds the worst day (−33k→−19–26k) but barely moves MaxDD. Root cause is **structural, not theta**: a put is short the sleeve's own long-NIFTY-drift edge — the 2% OTM hedge loses −₹185k on the 80 up-days (when the sleeve wins) and recovers only +₹30k on the 67 down-days, profitable on just 24% of days. You pay premium every session to bet against your own direction; it only pays on rare gaps and, being OTM, covers ~25–30% of a bad day. Combined with the sleeve's own weekly-CALL return-vehicle failure (theta 54.5% of premium) and R33/R36, **option buying in any role loses on this family; the only wrapper that works is long delta-1 overnight, and the tail is best de-risked by sizing, not options.** Report: `docs/research/strategy/futures_follow_cap50/2026-07-13_otm_put_overnight_hedge_backtest.md`.

### News-announcement spikes MEAN-REVERT at retail latency — momentum-chasing has no edge, at any category or speed (R43, 2026-07-07)

Tested the "trade the stocks in the news" thesis end-to-end before building any live plumbing: 12 months of NSE corporate announcements (181,536; symbol+category+second-resolution timestamps from the `corporate-announcements` JSON API) × Zerodha 1m prices, 13,590 eligible symbol-days, 108-cell grid (detection latency 30/90/300s × price-reaction 1/2/3% × time-of-day-adjusted volume-surge 1.5/2.5/4× × 4 exits, real MIS/CNC charges). **Every cell is net-negative and the loss is GROSS** (best cell −0.66%/trade; charges add only ~0.15pp). Mechanism: after a confirmed ≥2% spike in the news direction, single names revert ~1% by the same close (medians −1.1 to −1.6%); requiring volume-surge ≥2.5× — the intuitive "confirmation" — selects HARDER reversals (−1.15 to −1.46%). No category survives, not even order-wins (n=393, T+1 −0.35%, median −0.96%); latency is irrelevant (30s ≈ 300s) — the edge doesn't decay across our range, it never exists at retail-reachable timescales. Third independent confirmation of the same structural fact (R42 short-mirror bounce, MIS-T+0 late-momentum fade): **Indian single-name spikes mean-revert at intraday-to-T+1 horizons; the only wrapper that works on spike-adjacent signals is LONG delta-1 held overnight on *pre-close* strength, not chase-entries after the move.** Inverse lead parked: fading the ≥2%+vol-surge pop shorts ~+1.2–1.5% gross to EOD (n≈1,700) → ~+0.5–0.9% net — execution-fragile smallcap shorting, needs its own round before any belief. Report: `docs/research/strategy/news_event_study/2026-07-07_r43_news_momentum_event_study.md`. Issue #361.

**R44 addendum (2026-07-07): delayed pattern entries don't rescue it either.** All 5 defined-risk long entries on the same tape reject — bull-flag pullback with an RR≥1.5/2.0 feasibility gate (25% wins where RR 2 needs ~33%), the simplified engine's no-supply/demand-return 5m entry (21% wins — the structure doesn't transfer off F&O large caps), VWAP reclaim, opening-range breakout, and pre-close-strength→overnight. The last one matters most: the wrapper that clears 12%+ CAGR on sector-confirmed signals is **exactly zero** conditioned on news (+0.02%/trade, n=135) — a news catalyst is *adverse selection* relative to sector confirmation (the stock is up because of the crowd, not because its sector carries it). Entry timing was never the problem; post-news drift is negative-to-zero at every structure tested. Report: `docs/research/strategy/news_event_study/2026-07-07_r44_delayed_entry_patterns.md`. Issue #363.

**R45 addendum (2026-07-07): the multi-day "PEAD" horizon doesn't rescue it either — and pooled event-study tables on this universe are structurally untrustworthy without a split-half check.** Day-0 reaction × T+1..T+20 NIFTY-adjusted drift looks like textbook PEAD pooled (t-stats up to 4.6) yet EVERY reaction bucket flips sign between independent halves: the event universe is smallcap-tilted, so "abnormal return" vs NIFTY is mostly the smallcap segment cycle. Methodological rule for future event studies on broad-universe announcements: (a) benchmark against a size-matched index or the event universe's own neutral bucket, not NIFTY alone; (b) no pooled t-stat is meaningful without half-consistency. The two half-consistent facts: big down-reactions underperform peers ~−1.3..−1.6pp/20d (short-side, non-shortable smallcaps), and SAST filings carry +1.2..+2.5% AR@10 in both halves — but filing summaries are direction-blind boilerplate, so the literature-backed **promoter-BUYING drift remains untested** (needs PDF parsing for direction+size; the best-qualified future news round). Volume confirmation was anti-selective for the third consecutive round — treat "volume surge" as a crowd/adverse-selection marker on news names, not confirmation. Report: `docs/research/strategy/news_event_study/2026-07-07_r45_multi_horizon_drift.md`. Issue #368.

### The BUY-mirror's full-day volume gate is selectivity disguised as latency (R48, 2026-07-07)

The chartink-mirror BUY rule's gates 2/8 (running cumvol > SMA50/SMA200 of FULL-day volumes) are the last gate to clear on 89.8% of PASS days (median +145 min after the rest of the stack; median first-PASS 13:30 IST). A time-of-day-adjusted gate (`cumvol >= K×f(t)×SMA`) recovers ~2h and +0.7pp/signal on true-PASS days — but the added fires are 1:1 negative-EV morning spike-then-fades in both halves at every K, and the junk *worsens* as the early-relative-volume bar rises. Two durable lessons: (1) forcing running volume to beat a full-day average is a de facto "sustained volume trend day" filter — removing it for latency destroys the selection; (2) **extreme early relative-volume surges are anti-selective on the screened F&O universe too**, extending R43's news-smallcap finding. Any latency fix must be a *second tier* (early-watch/pre-arm), not a gate replacement. Also: live first-PASS timestamps on app-restart days are boot-clamped, not gate-timing evidence (the NAUKRI-11:35 anecdote was partly the 11:30:58 restart). Report: `docs/research/strategy/screener/2026-07-07_r48_time_of_day_volume_gate.md`.
